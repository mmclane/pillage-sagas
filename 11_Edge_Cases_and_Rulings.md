# 11 — Edge Cases and Rulings

Proactive rulings on ambiguous interactions between the campaign's many systems. Compiled by reading the rules carefully; expect this file to grow as actual play reveals new questions.

Organized by the system the question is *about*, not where the rule lives.

---

## Named Characters and Chieftain designation

### A Named Character on Death's Door or Infection — can they be designated Chieftain?

No. A character whose chronic-condition recovery roll hasn't resolved cannot deploy, and therefore cannot be Chieftain. They sit out battles until they recover.

### The Chieftain dies mid-battle. Can another Character become Chieftain mid-game?

No. The Chieftain designation is locked at the start of a battle and persists for that game. If the Chieftain falls, their Talents stop functioning (no Chieftain to anchor them), but no other character becomes Chieftain until the next battle's army-build step.

### A Character dies as Chieftain. Does the free Title roll for the next Chieftain apply once, or every time a Chieftain dies?

It applies **once per death**. The next Chieftain designation after a Chieftain's death triggers one Title roll. If that successor Chieftain also dies in their first battle, the next successor gets another Title roll. The free Title is a recognition of fresh leadership, not a one-time campaign event.

### Can a Specialist (Healer / Banner-bearer / Warhorn-bearer) become Chieftain?

No. The Chieftain must be a Named Character (per [01_Core_Rules.md §3b](01_Core_Rules.md)). Specialists are locked to their roles and never lead the warband. If all Characters die and no replacements are recruited, the warband cannot field a battle.

## XP, Talents, and Advancement

### A character with both Iron Stance and Bone Density Skills — both grant re-rolls on defence. Can they be used in the same battle?

Yes. They are separate Skills with separate triggers (Iron Stance is melee defence, Bone Density is ranged defence). A character may use one of each per game. They do not stack on the same defence roll, however.

### A character gains a Talent while their warband already has a character with that Talent.

This shouldn't happen — Talents are unique within the warband ([02 §4](02_XP_and_Advancement.md)). If it somehow does (e.g., a recruited character was previously someone else's), the duplicate is discarded with full XP refund.

### A Berserker-equipped figure also has the Berserker's Echo Skill — does the Skill effect stack with Berserker rage?

Yes. Berserker's Echo grants the Inspiring trait while engaged in melee. Berserker rage (game-long or one-turn via mushrooms) is a separate set of bonuses (auto-pass morale, +1 attack). Both apply to the figure simultaneously.

### A character with Long Strides (+1" movement) is equipped as a Berserker that game — do they get the Berserker's movement profile plus Long Strides?

Yes. Long Strides is a permanent personal stat improvement that applies to whatever role the character is in. Stack with Berserker's printed stats up to the +2 cumulative cap on movement.

## Boasts

### A multi-game Boast 🌙 is tied to a specific Named Character (Threefold Saga, Hall of the Fallen). That character dies before completing.

The Boast is discarded with no Renown awarded. The player draws a fresh Boast at the start of their next battle as normal. They do not get a free re-draw — losing the multi-game Boast is part of the risk of choosing it.

### Boast completion in the moment of a Confusion roll

If a figure under mushroom Confusion (rolled 1) charges and kills the nearest figure (friend or foe), does that satisfy Boasts that require killing an enemy?

Yes if the killed figure was an enemy. The mushroom user is still your figure; their actions count as yours regardless of confusion. If they kill a friendly figure, that does not satisfy any Boast (you don't kill your own warband for credit).

### Skald's Ear (Skill) and a Boast whose trigger was missed several turns ago

Skald's Ear allows claiming a Boast on the turn following the trigger, up to the end of that next turn. It does NOT extend further back. If you missed claiming a Boast 3 turns ago, it's gone — Skald's Ear is for the most recent turn.

### First Blood (C14) when both warbands kill in the same turn

If both warbands kill a figure in the same turn (one in shooting phase, one in melee phase), the *order of resolution* per Pillage's standard turn structure determines "first." Shooting resolves before melee in Pillage's turn order, so whichever side's shooting kill came first wins First Blood.

### Roving Hounds kills count for First Blood?

No. First Blood is "the first enemy figure killed in the game (across both warbands) is killed by one of your figures." Roving Hounds are not anyone's figures (ownerless). Hound-kills do not trigger First Blood for any player. If a hound kills first, no one earns First Blood that game.

### Sacred Stone Twist + a Boast that requires killing an enemy

A kill in base contact with the Sacred Stone counts for any Boast that requires killing an enemy. The Sacred Stone's +1 Renown bonus stacks with any Boast Renown.

## Stores

### Hallucinogenic Mushrooms used by a Berserker-equipped figure

The Berserker-equipped figure may use the mushroom Store on top of their normal Berserker abilities. Per [04 Part A](04_Stores_and_Relics.md), the boost stacks once, but they must still roll for Confusion.

Specifically: the Berserker's rage state is game-long once triggered (per Pillage's rules). The Store's one-turn boost is additive (auto-pass morale even on a 1 already covered, +1 additional attack for that turn). On a Confusion 1 result, the figure goes Wild for that turn — overriding the normal Berserker rage benefits for that turn only. Game-long rage resumes the turn after.

### A figure with Captured Saint's Bone Store dies before triggering it

Lost. The Store was issued to that figure at army-build; it's gone when they die. The bone falls to the battlefield as flavor; it does not become recoverable loot.

### Snares + cavalry charging through them

A cavalry figure that moves over a snare during a charge takes the halved-movement effect immediately. If this means the charge can no longer reach the target, the charge fails (per Pillage's failed charge rules).

## Relics

### A Relic's wielder dies in battle. The killing warband flees / routs immediately after.

The Relic drops at the spot of the wielder's death and is loot until game end. If no figure carries it off the table by then, the Relic returns to its original owner's warband. The killing warband's rout does not change this.

### A figure carrying a Relic loot token is captured alive

The captor controls both the wielder AND the dropped Relic. In the post-game Ransom step, the captor may demand the Relic per the standard rules. Note that the Relic is no longer formally assigned to the original wielder (the wielder is captured, not killed), so on ransom the Relic may be returned with the wielder unless the captor demands otherwise.

### Two warbands both want a Relic from a Sack the Monastery battle and both attackers somehow win (multiplayer scenario)

In standard 1v1 battles this doesn't happen. In Custom Battle Types with multiple attackers, the campaign organiser awards the Relic to the warband whose figure carried it off first. Disputed cases: roll-off.

## Holdings

### A Holding's source Battle Type and the Custom Battle Type mechanic — can I use a Custom Battle Type to win a Holding?

Yes, if the Custom Battle Type maps to the Holding reward category. The Holding's name is chosen by the winner at victory. For example: a Custom "Burn the Witch's Grove" with Holding reward category could grant a custom Holding ("Witch's Grove +20 gp/Winter"), but constrained by the standard caps in [05_Scenarios.md](05_Scenarios.md) Custom Battle Types rules.

### A player with a Watch Tower Holding wins another Coast Watch Tower battle

They cannot hold two Watch Towers (max 1 per type). They must either decline the new Holding (still earn the battle's standard rewards) or destroy and replace the old one with the new one (rename, refresh).

### Holdings yield timing on a battle that ended in a draw

A draw is not a win. Holdings remain held; no flips happen. Both warbands still receive their currently-held Holdings' yield per the campaign cadence.

### A Holding-holder is challenged but the challenger's proposal is accepted as a non-Holding Battle Type

The Holding isn't contested in that battle. The Holding remains held by its current owner. To contest a Holding, the challenger must propose the specific source Battle Type (or a Custom Battle Type with the Holding reward category).

## Saga Roll and Saga Injury Table

### A Feast spend on a character who has both Death's Door (slot 4) and Infection (slot 8) outcomes simultaneously

The +1 to the next d6 recovery roll applies to **one** condition per Feast (player's choice). To clear both, two Feasts are needed (one per condition), spread across post-games.

### A Healer mitigation re-roll on a Saga Injury — can the re-roll target the Healer themselves?

No (per [06 §1 Interactions](06_Tables.md)): a wounded Healer cannot re-roll their own injury. The re-roll benefits another character.

### Saga Roll result "Wandering Berserker" → free Named Character — what role do they take?

Under the v0.3 class-flex model, this entry adds a free Named Character. They start with 0 XP and no advancements. (The original Berserker class no longer exists as a Named Character class; if a player wants the new Character to focus on Berserker-style play, they can equip them as a Berserker each battle.)

### Wyrd is Cruel (Saga Roll 70-71) — can it cascade?

Yes. If you roll Wyrd is Cruel and then roll Wyrd is Cruel again on the re-roll, roll a third time. Apply the worst of the three. The cascade is rare but theoretically unbounded.

## Annual Events

### A player misses a meeting when an Annual Event fires. Do they still suffer / benefit?

Yes. Annual Events affect every warband in the campaign, including those who didn't field a battle that meeting. Apply effects to their warband at the next opportunity. Mitigation Renown can still be spent retroactively if they have Renown to spend.

### A late-joining player suffers Annual Events that fired before they joined?

No. Late joiners are not retroactively affected. They start fresh from the next Annual Event after they join.

### A New Hall Rises (AE29) — what counts as "the first" Sack the Hall victory?

The first **successful** Sack the Hall battle completed by any warband across the campaign after AE29 fires. Whoever wins it gets the doubled Burned Hall Holding. After that warband wins it, AE29's effect is consumed; subsequent Sack the Hall victories yield standard Burned Hall Holdings.

### Civil War (AE16) — does the defecting character keep their wielded Relic?

Yes. Per the card text, the departing character takes any wielded Relic with them to their new warband. The original warband loses the Relic permanently.

### Old Curses Wake (AE22) — if my Chieftain just died, who rolls?

The character who would be Chieftain in your next battle. If you have multiple Characters and haven't decided who'll be Chieftain, designate now before rolling.

## Composition and proposals

### Custom Battle Type with both players in the same role (both raiders)

Allowed if both players agree. The "role" is the in-fiction framing; mechanically, both sides may have similar objectives. Useful for "Race for the Loot" or "Mutual Raid" scenarios. The campaign organiser may want to set objective parity (both sides need to extract X loot off opposite edges; first to extract more wins).

### A player proposes a Battle Type but the target has banned the Location

The Location ban is mutual at campaign start ([01_Core_Rules.md §3](01_Core_Rules.md) and [09_Quick_Start.md](09_Quick_Start.md)). The challenger must propose a different Location (which the target either accepts or counter-proposes against). The ban cannot be bypassed by Twists or other means.

### Refusing a proposal while you already have an active proposal of your own to someone else

Allowed. You may refuse incoming proposals while pursuing your own. Refusal carries the standard penalty (-1 Renown, locked out of being a target until you propose). Pursuing your own proposal doesn't satisfy "propose at least one Battle Type" — you must successfully propose AND have the proposal accepted to unlock yourself as a target again.

### A target who refuses a proposal AND has not yet proposed this season — what happens?

The standard refusal penalty applies (-1 Renown). The "must propose to be a target again" rule means until they propose at least one Battle Type that gets accepted (clean, with Twist, or counter-proposed back), no one can target them. They can still be in the campaign and earn Renown via Annual Events and Holdings yield.

### Counter-amend on a Custom Battle Type — how many rounds?

Up to 2 rounds, per [05 §3 Custom Battle Types](05_Scenarios.md). After 2 rounds of amendment without agreement, both players either accept a final form or refuse the engagement.

## Multi-warband interactions

### A Bitter Enmity mark exists between two characters. Their warbands face each other again — does the mark still apply?

Yes. Bitter Enmity is permanent (per the Saga Injury Table). The character with the mark has +1 to hit rolls against any figure from the targeted warband, for the rest of the campaign. It's a personal vendetta, not a single-game effect.

### Two characters in the same warband both have Bitter Enmity marks against the same enemy warband

Both apply independently. Each character with a mark gets the +1 against figures from the targeted warband. They do not stack on the same enemy figure (max +1).

### A Saga of Survival 🌙 multi-game Boast holder is Chieftain in two consecutive battles and goes Down in the third

The streak resets. They must start a new 3-game streak to claim Saga of Survival.

## Grand Finale

### A character on Death's Door / Infection at the Grand Finale — can they deploy?

No. Chronic conditions don't clear in time. The character sits out the Finale. After the Finale, the campaign ends; if they hadn't recovered, they're considered missed (no further rolls).

### Holdings yield at the Grand Finale

Holdings yield one final time at the end of the Grand Finale battle, before final Renown is tallied. This is the "season-end" payout if your cadence is per-Winter rather than per-post-game.

### Tied Renown at season's end in Feuds Mode

Tiebreakers, in order:
1. Most completed Legendary Boasts.
2. Most Titles held by the warband (any character).
3. Most Holdings currently controlled.
4. Coin flip.

### Tied side Renown at season's end in Two Sides Mode

Tiebreakers, in order:
1. Total Holdings across all warbands on the side.
2. Total Legendary Boasts completed across the side.
3. Total Titles held across the side.
4. Coin flip.

### A rule I think is unfair or unfun

Discuss with the campaign organiser. The doc is authoritative but house rules are encouraged when both players (or the whole club) agree. Note such house rules in the campaign organiser's master sheet for consistency.
